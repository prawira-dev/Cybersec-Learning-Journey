# SOC Fundamentals
## Intro
* **Overview:** Introduces the concept of a Security Operations Center (SOC) as a dedicated defensive security facility.
* **Core Purpose:** To continuously monitor an organization's network, systems, and confidential data 24 hours a day, 7 days a week to detect suspicious activity and prevent cyber threats before they cause significant damage.
* **Key Learning Objectives:**
    * Establishing a baseline understanding of what a SOC is.
    * Exploring detection and response workflows.
    * Examining the fundamental pillars of a SOC: People, Processes, and Technology.
    * Applying concepts through a practical exercise.

## Purpose and Components
* **Core Focus:** The primary mission of a SOC team is to keep Detection and Response intact by utilizing centralized security solutions to continuously monitor an organization's network and systems.
* **Detection Capabilities**:
    * **Detect Vulnerabilities**: Identifying software weaknesses or unpatched systems (e.g., MS Windows devices missing critical patches) that attackers could exploit.
    * **Detect Unauthorized Activity**: Catching unauthorized logins or abused credentials quickly (often flagged using contextual clues like geographic anomalies) before damage occurs.
    * **Detect Policy Violations**: Ensuring compliance with internal rules and procedures (such as preventing users from downloading pirated media or insecurely sharing confidential files).
    * **Detect Intrusions**: Identifying unauthorized access to systems or networks, such as exploited web applications or malware infections resulting from malicious link visits.
* **Response Capabilities**: Assisting the incident response team by taking immediate steps to minimize the impact of detected incidents and conducting root cause analysis.
* **The Three Pillars of a SOC**: A mature and efficient SOC relies on the coexistence of three foundational pillars: People, Process, and Technology.

## People
* **Overview:** Explores the People pillar of a SOC, emphasizing that human analysts are critical for filtering out false positives ("noise") generated by automated security tools and identifying genuinely harmful threats.
* **Hierarchy and Roles of the SOC Team**:
    * **SOC Analyst (Level 1)**: The first responders who handle initial alert triage to determine if a detection is harmful and report it through proper channels.
    * **SOC Analyst (Level 2)**: Investigates complex alerts deeper and correlates data across multiple log sources.
    * **SOC Analyst (Level 3)**: Senior professionals who proactively hunt for threats and handle critical incident responses, including containment, eradication, and recovery.
    * **Security Engineer**: Responsible for deploying, configuring, and maintaining the security solutions used by the team.
    * **Detection Engineer**: Focuses on building, tuning, and establishing the detection rules behind security tools.
    * **SOC Manager**: Oversees team processes, manages resources, and communicates the organization's security posture to the CISO.

## Process
* **Overview:** Explores the standard workflows and operational processes followed by a SOC team to handle alerts, structure reporting, and investigate security incidents.
* **Alert Triage:** The foundational process of analyzing incoming security alerts to determine their severity, prioritize them, and answer the 5 Ws:
    * **What?** Identifies the specific activity or malicious behavior (e.g., a malicious file detected).
    * **When?** Records the precise timestamp of the event.
    * **Where?** Pinpoints the affected host, directory, or network endpoint.
    * **Who?** Identifies the user account or entity associated with the activity.
    * **Why?** Determines the root cause or motive behind the event (e.g., downloading files from a pirated software site).
* **Reporting:** Escalating harmful detections to higher-level analysts or management via tickets. Reports must detail all 5 Ws, include thorough analysis, and provide screenshot evidence.
* **Incident Response and Forensics:** Escalating critical security detections to specialized response teams to minimize impact, perform root cause analysis, and examine system or network artifacts.

## Technology
* **Overview:** Explores the Technology pillar of a SOC, focusing on the specialized security solutions that centralize network information, automate detection capabilities, and minimize manual workloads for analysts.
* **Key Security Solutions:**
    * **SIEM (Security Information and Event Management):** Collects logs from diverse network log sources, correlates data, and triggers alerts based on predefined detection rules (along with modern machine learning, user behavior analytics, and threat intelligence capabilities). Note: SIEM solutions focus strictly on detection.
    * **EDR (Endpoint Detection and Response):** Operates at the endpoint level to provide real-time and historical device visibility, deep investigation capabilities, and automated responses.
    * **Firewall:** Acts as a network security barrier between internal networks and external environments (like the Internet) by monitoring, filtering, and blocking unauthorized incoming and outgoing traffic.
    * **Additional Technologies:** Mentions other key solutions integrated into mature SOC ecosystems, such as Antivirus, EPP, IDS/IPS, XDR, and SOAR.

## Practical Exercise of SOC
* **Overview:** Provides a hands-on practical lab simulating the role of a Level 1 SOC Analyst investigating a port scanning alert using an interactive SIEM interface.
* **Scenario Details:**
    * **Alert:** Port scanning activity detected on a network host.
    * **Context:** The vulnerability assessment team previously notified the SOC that they were conducting an authorized port scan from the host 10.0.0.8.
* **Key Investigation Steps (The 5 Ws & Notes):**
    * **What:** Identifies the triggering activity (port scan).
    * **When:** Records the precise timestamp of the activity.
    * **Where:** Identifies the destination host IP address.
    * **Who:** Identifies the source host name.
    * **Why:** Determines whether the activity was intended (legitimate internal assessment) or malicious.
    * **Additional Notes:** Checks whether any response traffic was sent back to the scanning IP address.
* **Completion:** Successfully analyzing the logs, answering the 5 Ws, and closing out the alert reveals the final completion flag.
