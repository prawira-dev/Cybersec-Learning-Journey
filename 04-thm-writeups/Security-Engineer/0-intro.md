# Security Engineer Intro
## Task 1: Intro
* **Overview:** Introduces the Security Engineer Intro room on TryHackMe, outlining the vital role security engineers play as the backbone of an enterprise's overall cybersecurity posture.
* **Learning Objectives:** Sets the foundation for exploring why security engineers are needed, the qualifications required to enter the field, and what a typical day of work looks like.
* **Prerequisites:** Recommends completing the foundational Pre Security path before diving deeper into the room's core engineering concepts.

## Task 2: What is a Security Engineer?
* **Overview:** Defines the primary role, expectations, and core qualifications of a security engineer within an organization.
* **Key Responsibilities:**
    * Owns the overall security of the organization and acts as the main person responsible for securing digital assets.
    * Ensures that cybersecurity risk is minimized at all times.
    * Devises strategies and builds systems to minimize threats.
    * Periodically conducts tests to evaluate the security posture, identify vulnerabilities, and prepare mitigations.
    * Develops secure network solutions and architectures for reliable, trustworthy systems.
    * Collaborates across multiple teams to establish unified security protocols.
* **Basic Qualifications Required:**
    * 0 to 2 years of experience in IT administration, helpdesk support, networks, or security operations.
    * Foundational understanding of operating systems, computer networks, and programming.
    * Basic familiarity with security concepts such as Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC).

## Task 3: Core Responsibilities of a Security Engineer
* **Overview:** Expands on the general expectations and foundational duties an organization places on a security engineer to safeguard its digital environment.
* **Asset Management / Asset Inventory:**
    * Maintains an up-to-date and comprehensive inventory of all organizational digital assets.
    * Tracks vital information including asset names, types, IP addresses, physical and network locations, running applications, access permissions (internal vs. public-facing), and designated asset owners.
* **Security Policies:**
    * Helps formulate and implement robust organization-wide security policies based on established Security Principles.
    * Manages policy exceptions driven by business needs by consulting security principles to evaluate risks and propose mitigating steps.
* **Secure by Design:**
    * Focuses on the philosophy that embedding security from the ground up delivers the highest Return on Investment (ROI).
    * Involves establishing a Secure Network Architecture, hardening Windows, Linux, and Active Directory environments, and embedding security into the Secure Software Development Lifecycle.
* **Security Assessment and Assurance:**
    * Acknowledges that security requires continuous effort rather than a one-time setup.
    * Coordinates and schedules regular security assessments, audits, red-teaming, and purple-teaming exercises, while prioritizing and implementing findings from external partners.

## Task 4: Continuous Improvement
* **Overview:** Explores the ongoing cycle of improvement required by security engineers to maintain a strong security posture as organizations evolve over time.
* **Key Focus Areas:**
    * **Ensuring Awareness:** Running security awareness training to educate employees on avoiding social engineering attacks, addressing the reality that humans are often considered the weakest link in an organization's security.
    * **Managing Risks:** Identifying security risks, assessing their likelihood and impact, and acting as a trusted advisor to management on whether to accept or mitigate those risks.
    * **Change Management:** Tracking changes to digital assets—such as upgrading e-commerce modules—and ensuring they undergo risk assessments, penetration testing, and vulnerability assessments before deployment.
    * **Vulnerability Management:** Continuously monitoring for vulnerabilities across the organization's software and planning patches based on severity.
    * **Compliance and Audits:** Working with internal and external auditors to ensure adherence to standards like PCI-DSS, HIPAA, SOC2, ISO27001, and NIST-800-53, while maintaining security certifications.

## Task 5: Additional Roles and Responsibilities
* **Overview:** Explores additional cross-functional duties that security engineers often take on beyond their core scope to support overall organizational readiness.
* **Managing Security Tooling:** Involves configuring, fine-tuning, or managing security solutions such as SIEMs, Firewalls, WAFs, and EDRs, as well as providing technical evaluations and recommendations for new security tools.
* **Tabletop Exercises:** Conducting simulated, discussion-based scenarios (such as an endpoint compromise via phishing) to test and evaluate the operational readiness and response playbooks of the security team.
* **Disaster Recovery and Crisis Management:** Assisting with business continuity and disaster recovery planning to ensure the organization can maintain operations during major security incidents or crises.

## Task 6: Walking in Their Shoes
* **Overview:** Provides a scenario-based practical walkthrough to illustrate how a security engineer handles day-to-day security challenges in a real-world enterprise environment.
* **Key Focus Areas:**
    * Applying security principles to investigate alerts and triage vulnerabilities.
    * Collaborating with IT and administrative teams to implement patches without disrupting business workflows.
    * Balancing practical engineering constraints with strict compliance and risk management requirements.
